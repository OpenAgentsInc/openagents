## Is greedy decoding deterministic?

Eight greedy calls on one question returned 1 distinct answer: `billing` x8.

Greedy repeats itself in one process. That makes L1 reproducible run to run on one host.

## Does a seed reproduce a sample?

Seeds `0..16` were drawn twice in one process. 16 of 16 agreed.

The same seeds in a second helper process agreed with the first on 16 of 16.

A recorded seed reproduces a sample, in process and across processes. An L2 estimate can honestly claim reproducibility.

## What this record does not settle

- The context limit. Driving a state past the window costs minutes of sampling and is worth its own run.
- Whether the runtime reports token counts. The helper does not surface any, so `usage` stays empty rather than carrying a character-count fiction.
- Anything about calibration. Every number here is behavior, not accuracy against labelled outcomes at a size worth fitting.

## What this changes

Written after the run, against the numbers above.

Two results land the way the design hoped, and two do not.

**Reproducibility is better than assumed.** Greedy repeats itself, and a
recorded seed reproduces its sample both inside one helper process and across
a fresh one. Every estimator here can honestly claim its numbers are
replayable, which is more than the design asked for.

**The distribution is nearly degenerate, and that is the problem.** The L2
estimator rests on sampling spread carrying some signal about difficulty. It
does not. Top share runs 0.81 to 1.00 across every item, easy and hard alike,
and the mean gap between the two buckets is 0.03. Worse, the two items the
model gets wrong are held at 0.81 — as consistently as the items it gets
right. That is the failure the architecture page warned about in the
abstract, observed concretely: **the model is unanimous and wrong, and
unanimity is not evidence.**

An estimator whose output lives in a narrow band near 1.0 cannot be
calibrated into a useful probability. There is no dynamic range to map. A
fitted map would compress 0.81 and 1.00 onto two nearby numbers and tell a
caller nothing about which answers to trust.

**The certainty band is constant.** Every one of the eight items returned
`likely`, including both wrong answers. As prompted against the base model,
the band carries no signal at all, so L3 has nothing to fit either.

**Order sensitivity is worse than kev's.** Reversing the option list flipped
one of eight greedy answers, 12% against kev-0.5b's 7.4% over four
permutations and hosted Jev's zero observed flips.

### The consequence

The honest disposition for a first Lev version follows directly:

- **L1 is what ships.** A typed choice, its shape guaranteed by constrained
  decoding, deterministic, free, private, and fast. That is a real product
  and it is the thing the router lane in this repository's history was
  already using without calling it one.
- **L2 and L3 do not ship a probability.** Neither produces a signal with
  enough range to calibrate, so a Lev door refuses `uncalibrated` rather than
  reporting a number. The refusal was designed as a guard against skipping
  the measurement; it turns out to be the measured answer.
- **Issues #9350 and #9352 are blocked**, not merely unscheduled. Fitting a
  calibration map needs a raw signal that varies with correctness, and there
  is not one yet. The next move is an adapter trained to select bands against
  labelled outcomes — which is #9353, and which needs Apple's toolkit.

This is the stop condition #9346 was written with: *if sampling spread
carries no signal about difficulty, say so plainly in the record and stop the
sequence for a design review.* This section is that.

### A confound in this record

The guardrail probe put `yes` first in a two-option Choice and every item
answered `yes`, including the ordinary control. Given the first-option
preference measured in the isolation probe, that table shows the model
answering position rather than content, so it is evidence that guardrails did
not fire and no evidence at all about judgment. A rerun should randomize the
option order per item.
