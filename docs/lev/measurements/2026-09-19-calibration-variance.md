## Why this run exists

[`2026-09-19-seed-variance.md`](2026-09-19-seed-variance.md) measured the
block-to-block spread of accuracy and of the mean top share, and nothing
else. `crates/gym/src/ab.rs` turned the accuracy number into the bar a win
has to clear and recorded the other three metrics as `unmeasured`, which
means a win on ECE, Brier, or log loss reported `unverifiable` and could
never earn a keep.

That is not a tidiness problem. The largest result claimed this week is a
calibration result: conditioning the map on a trained certainty band cut log
loss from 2.601 to 0.388 where a pooled map did not help at all. Under the
gate as it stood, an 85% reduction and a 1% one were treated the same way,
because neither had a floor to be measured against.

This run measures those floors.

## What is measured, and what "pooled" means

Accuracy, Brier, and log loss are means over items. Resampling seeds changes
each item's contribution, the mean moves, and the spread of that mean is
what a gate wants.

**ECE is not a mean over items.** It is computed inside ten fixed bins: each
bin contributes the gap between its mean reported probability and its share
correct, weighted by how many items landed in it. Changing the seed block
moves an ECE two ways at once — the values move, and items cross bin edges,
which regroups the terms. Because each bin takes an absolute value,
regrouping changes how much the errors inside a bin cancel. A spread taken
over blocks mixes the two effects, and a floor derived from it without
saying so would look rigorous and would not be.

So both are reported, under different names.

- **Live ECE** is what `gym::calibrate::score` computes: each block bins its
  own values. It is what every published number in this directory is, and it
  is what a gate compares. **Its spread is the floor.**
- **Frozen ECE** bins every item by its *mean* signal over all blocks and
  holds that membership fixed while each block's own values and outcomes are
  scored inside it. No block is privileged and nothing regroups, so its
  spread is the movement of the values alone. It says how much of the live
  spread is the binning rather than the door.

There is a second ambiguity in the word *pooled*, and it is the one
openagents#9372 found. **A suite ECE is not the item-weighted mean of its
families' ECEs**, because the bins are formed inside whatever set is scored:
two families that each carry an ECE of 0.25 can score zero together, when
one is over-confident inside a bin and the other is under-confident by as
much. Two different objects are both called a pooled ECE here:

- **The suite ECE** — every evaluation item in one ten-bin table. This is
  what `gym eval` and `lev-band` report, and what every published ECE on
  this suite is.
- **The A/B's pooled ECE** — which does not exist. `ab::pool` refuses to
  produce one whenever more than one family is pooled, and `Rule::read_gain`
  says so by name. The only ECE an A/B round ever reads is a single
  family's.

Both are measured here, and the floor the rule takes is discussed against
both at the end.

## How the draws were taken

`lev-calibration-sweep` asks the door and writes one line per item per
block, to `crates/gym/results/support-v2-calibration-blocks.jsonl`, which is
committed. `gym spread` reads those lines and produces everything below. No
door is asked to produce the document, so an error in the analysis costs a
recompile rather than another run of the hardware, and every number here can
be recomputed from the file.

Every draw is an L2 estimate over 8 samples from one seed block, on the same
98 evaluation items of `support-v2` that openagents#9370 swept, through the
same estimator, on the same machine. Block `b` draws seeds `b * 8 .. b * 8 +
8`, so no two blocks share a seed and rerunning a block is not a second
trial. The certainty band comes from one greedy call per item; greedy
decoding does not read the seed block, so the band is drawn once per door
and carried.

The passes are block-major: each block is one complete pass over the items,
in suite order. That is the order openagents#9370 drew in, and it means an
interrupted run leaves whole blocks rather than a grid with holes.
`Draws::complete` refuses to report on a grid that has holes anyway, and
names the block and the first item missing from it.

**This reads items `support-v2-three-way` locks, and here is the count.**
The two suite files name the same 196 items under two partitionings, so a
tool reading the older one can spend the newer one's locked partition.
`lev-band` and `training/lev-adapter/convert.py` hold those items back; this
sweep does not. Of `support-v2`'s 98 evaluation items, **19 are locked by
the three-way file**, and 20 of its 98 calibration items are.

The reason is what a floor is for. Every claim this record judges was made
on those 98 items, and a floor measured on a different 79 of them is not the
floor those claims need. Nothing here is fitted, tuned, or chosen: the door
does not change between blocks, so no decision can leak from the items into
a selection. It is still a read, and it is recorded as one.

It also makes no difference to the numbers. Scoring the same draws over only
the 79 items the three-way file leaves open gives, on the base door: an
accuracy spread of 0.0338 against 0.0197, an ECE spread of 0.0308 against
0.0266, a Brier spread of 0.0168 against 0.0119, and a log-loss spread of
0.6757 against 0.6428. Three of the four floors would be *wider* on the open
items alone, which is what fewer items does to a spread. The locked items
are not carrying the floor; dropping them would only make it larger and less
comparable.

**The blocks are not evenly divided between the two doors, and the reason is
the machine rather than the design.** The base door drew eight evaluation
blocks — the same eight openagents#9370 drew — and the band adapter four. An
adapted call costs several times what a base call costs on this runtime, and
the device was shared with other work throughout. Both splits also carry one
calibration block per door, which is what a map is fitted on.

## One map, scored on every evaluation block

The map is fitted once, on the calibration draws of one block, which is how every published map number here was produced. It is then scored on every evaluation block. The paired gain is what the map bought on that block, and the spread of the gain is how much of a published improvement is the seeds. The fitting seeds are held fixed, so this is not the whole spread a map claim carries.

A map is fitted per group: over every item for the suite row, and over one family's items for a family row, which is the shape the published per-family maps were fitted in.

## What this implies

Written after the run, against the numbers above.

**Both doors reproduce their published rows digit for digit, and the
accuracy floor reproduces too.** Block 0 of the base door gives accuracy
0.765, ECE 0.106, Brier 0.154, log loss 1.952, six confident errors, against
the 0.77 / 0.106 / 0.154 / 1.952 / 6 recorded in
[`2026-09-19-suite-v2-scores.md`](2026-09-19-suite-v2-scores.md). Block 0 of
the band adapter gives 0.878 / 0.102 / 0.114 / 2.601 / 9, against the 0.88 /
0.102 / 0.114 / 2.601 / 9 in
[`2026-09-19-adapter-band.md`](2026-09-19-adapter-band.md). Over all eight
blocks the base door's accuracy standard deviation is **0.0197**, which is
openagents#9370's number to four places. These are the same eight blocks
that run drew, so this checks the collection path rather than confirming the
measurement — but a second path written from scratch landing on the same
arithmetic is worth having.

**Three of the four calibration metrics behave nothing like accuracy.** On
the base door, accuracy moves about two points across blocks and ECE moves
about three. Brier moves one. Log loss moves two thirds of a nat, a third of
its own value, and the confident-error count runs 6, 6, 10, 4, 4, 10, 6, 4.
Nothing about the door, the items, or the machine differed between those
blocks. Only the seeds did.

**The raw log loss on this door is mostly a clamp, and the clamp is a
count.** `calibrate::score` floors a probability at `1e-12`, so an item the
door got wrong at a reported 1.000 contributes 27.63 to the sum, which is
0.28 of a 98-item mean. On block 0 six items did that, and they are **87% of
the whole log loss of 1.952**. An L2 estimate over eight samples reports a
multiple of an eighth and nothing between, so an item is either clamped or
it is not, and the raw log loss steps by 0.28 as items cross that edge. A
standard deviation of 0.64 is not a smooth statistic wobbling. It is a count
of four to ten wearing a decimal point.

That has a consequence for the rule. **Confident errors and log loss are the
same items on the raw signal.** A confident error is an item wrong at 0.9 or
above, and the only value an eight-sample estimate can report at or above
0.9 is 1.000. So `ab`'s hard floor and its third-ranked metric read one
event two ways. They separate as soon as a map is serving, because a map
moves values off 1.000: the same eight blocks give a mapped log loss with a
standard deviation of 0.018 against the raw signal's 0.643.

**"Confident errors must not rise" refuses on noise.** The count ran 6, 6,
10, 4, 4, 10, 6, 4 over eight blocks of an unchanged door: a standard
deviation of 2.49 and a range of six. The rule's hard floor allows no rise
at all. That is the safe direction for a floor and it is not a mistake, but
it is now a measured fact that this door fails that floor against itself
about as often as not, and that reading "6 against 8" as a difference is
reading the seeds.

**The adapted door moves less, which is the question this run was asked.**
Over four blocks the band adapter returned the *same accuracy in every
block* — 0.878 four times, where the base door ran 0.745 to 0.796 — and its
calibration metrics move at about a third to a half of the base door's:

| Metric | Base door, 8 blocks | Band adapter, 4 blocks | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `accuracy` | 0.0197 | 0.0000 | — |
| `ece` | 0.0266 | 0.0090 | 3.0 |
| `brier` | 0.0119 | 0.0038 | 3.1 |
| `nll` | 0.6428 | 0.3281 | 2.0 |
| `confident_errors` | 2.4928 | 1.2583 | 2.0 |

A sharper door has less room to move, and the floors a sharper door needs
are smaller than the ones this suite carries. Four blocks of an adapted door
and eight of a base one are both thin estimates of a standard deviation;
what they support is the ordering, not the ratios to two places.

**The ECE wrinkle is real and it does not point one way.** Live ECE bins
each block by its own values; frozen ECE bins every item by its mean signal
over all blocks and holds that membership fixed. If the spread were mostly
regrouping, the frozen spread would be much the smaller everywhere. It is
not:

| Door and group | Live ECE sd | Frozen ECE sd | What regrouping does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| base, all 98 items | 0.0266 | 0.0179 | **adds half again** |
| base, `routing` | 0.0136 | 0.0074 | adds most of it |
| base, `urgency` | 0.0393 | 0.0441 | absorbs a tenth |
| base, `severity` | 0.1061 | 0.0986 | adds a little |
| band, all 98 items | 0.0090 | 0.0074 | adds a fifth |

At the suite level and on `routing`, about a third to a half of the live
spread is items crossing a bin edge rather than values moving. On `urgency`
regrouping runs the other way and slightly stabilises the number, because an
item that leaves one bin joins another whose error points the opposite way.
Both effects are present, they do not cancel, and the live spread — which is
what a gate compares — carries both. That is the right number for a gate,
and it is not a clean measurement of the door.

**The families do not share a spread, and ECE is where that matters most.**
`severity`'s ECE moves nearly eight times as far as `routing`'s across the
same blocks, on 18 items against 50. `routing`'s log loss moves six times as
far as `severity`'s, because the confident errors are nearly all in
`routing`. One bound per metric cannot be right for all three.

**The seed floor is the smaller half, and by a wider margin than it is for
accuracy.** The item-sampling interval on the base door's suite ECE runs
0.085 to 0.200 against a block spread of 0.027; on Brier, 0.112 to 0.218
against 0.012. On the band adapter the gap is wider still. The estimator
reproduces across seed blocks far more closely than a 98-item suite
reproduces across item sets. **A calibration win that clears a floor here
has been shown not to be the seeds, and nothing else.**

## The floors this produces

Every floor is derived the same way: the standard deviation of that metric
across seed blocks, over the whole 98-item evaluation split, on the noisier
of the two doors measured. That is the derivation the accuracy floor already
used, applied to the other three. On all four metrics the noisier door is
the base.

| Metric | Base door, 8 blocks | Band adapter, 4 blocks | Floor adopted | Two sigma at one block a side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `accuracy` | 0.0197 | 0.0000 | **0.0197** | 0.056 |
| `ece` | 0.0266 | 0.0090 | **0.0266** | 0.075 |
| `brier` | 0.0119 | 0.0038 | **0.0119** | 0.034 |
| `nll` | 0.6428 | 0.3281 | **0.6428** | 1.818 |
| `confident_errors` | 2.4928 | 1.2583 | none: a hard floor is not a prize | — |

Carrying the noisier door's number makes the bound conservative for a
comparison between these two doors, because the standard deviation of a
difference is `sqrt(sigma_control^2 / b + sigma_candidate^2 / c)` and the
adapted door contributes about a tenth of it.

**What each substitution costs.** One bound is carried per metric, and the
A/B reads some of them per family:

- `ece` is only ever read per family, because `ab::pool` leaves a pooled ECE
  unknown whenever more than one family is pooled. The base door's family
  spreads are 0.0136, 0.0393, and 0.1061 around the suite's 0.0266. So the
  guard over-refuses on `severity` by four times, slightly under-refuses on
  `routing`, and a single-family round on `severity` could clear a bar
  smaller than its own noise. That is the gap `Rule::pending_measurements`
  records, and it is why the per-family numbers are in the tables above
  rather than only inside a bound.
- `brier` and `nll` are read at the suite level, where item weighting
  recovers them exactly, and per family by the guard. Every family's spread
  is larger than the suite's on both, so the guard over-refuses there, which
  is the safe direction.
- `confident_errors` stays out of `metric_order` and keeps its hard floor
  with no allowance. Its measured spread is in the table so a reader can see
  what that floor costs.

**`probability-v1` is not changed by this run, and it is the obvious next
change.** That gate's `min_ece_reduction` carries "nothing has measured the
variance; openagents#9370 does", and its `pending_measurement` names the
same issue. This run is that measurement for ECE and Brier. A tenth of an
ECE near 0.12 is 0.012, which is less than half of one block-to-block
standard deviation, so the inherited threshold is smaller than the noise it
was meant to exclude. Deriving those margins moves the gate digest that
every recorded calibration verdict names, so it is its own change and its
own re-judging of the record.

## Every calibration claim on this suite, against the floor

The floor measured here covers Lev's L2 estimator at eight samples on
`support-v2`, on this machine, at base model signature `9799725`. It does
not transfer to another suite, another estimator, or another model's
published numbers. Claims outside it are listed as outside it rather than
judged by a number that does not apply.

Two different bars apply, and which one a claim gets depends on what kind of
claim it is.

- **Two doors, one block each.** The difference carries both blocks' noise,
  so the bar is `2 * sigma * sqrt(2)` using the floors above.
- **One door, raw against a fitted map.** Both numbers come from the same
  block, so the gain is paired and its own spread is measured directly. That
  bar is two standard deviations of the gain, and it is much tighter.

## What this run does not cover

**Seed resampling, and nothing else.** Every block holds the item set, the
door, the machine, and the estimator fixed. A difference that clears a floor
here has been shown not to be the seeds. It has not been shown to hold on
items this suite does not contain, and the item-sampling intervals above are
several times the block spreads on every metric. The two statements are
different and only the first is a floor.

**Eight blocks and four blocks are thin.** A standard deviation from eight
draws carries about 27% of its own size in uncertainty, and one from four
draws about 41%. The floors should be read as the right order with a digit
of slack, not as four-place constants, and `ab::Rule` records them to four
places only because a digest has to be reproducible.

**One machine, one base model signature: `9799725`.** A map, an adapter, and
every threshold derived from them are void when the operating system moves
the base.

**The choice adapter and the permutation adapter were not swept.** Two doors
were. A floor is a property of the metric on this suite at this estimator
rather than of one door, and the two measured bracket the question — but a
claim about the choice adapter is judged here against a floor measured on
its siblings.

**The fitting seeds are held fixed.** Each map is fitted once, on the
calibration draws of block 0, and scored on every evaluation block. That is
the question a published map claim asks, and it is not the whole spread a
map carries: a fresh calibration block would refit the table as well as
rescore it. Measuring that needs calibration draws from more than one block,
which this run did not take.

**A paired item-level test would have more power than any of this.** Both
doors answer the same 98 items, so a test over per-item differences would
resolve smaller effects than a comparison of block means. The gate compares
block means, so that is what these floors are for; a sharper test is a
different change to `ab`.
