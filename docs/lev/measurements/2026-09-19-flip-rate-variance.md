# The spread a flip rate carries across option orders

Generated by `cargo run --release -p lev --bin lev-order-sweep`. Every number comes from the live on-device runtime on one machine.

The exact command:

```text
./scripts/build-lev-bridge.sh
cargo run --release -p lev --bin lev-order-sweep -- \
    --helpers 4 \
    --door lev-base= \
    --door lev-choice=~/code/lev-adapter-work/runs/lev-v1/lev.fmadapter \
    --door lev-band=~/code/lev-adapter-work/runs/lev-band/levband.fmadapter \
    --door lev-permutation=~/code/lev-adapter-work/runs/lev-perm/levperm.fmadapter
```

The flip rate this repository publishes is a greedy statistic over two option
orders: ask every Choice item in the suite's own order, ask it again
reversed, and count the items whose winning option changed. Greedy decoding
carries no seed, so the perturbation axis is the option order itself and the
seed sweep in [`2026-09-19-seed-variance.md`](2026-09-19-seed-variance.md)
says nothing about this number.

A three-option Choice has six orders and fifteen distinct pairs of them. The
published number is one of the fifteen. This sweep asks every item under all
six, so every pair's flip rate is computed rather than estimated, and the
spread over the fifteen is the whole population of two-order flip rates these
items can produce rather than a sample of it.

## What this implies

Written after the run, against the numbers above.

**The perturbation axis is the option order, and greedy decoding has no
other one.** A greedy call carries no seed — `bridge::Sampling::Greedy` has
no seed field to set — and the repeat pass confirms it: every door answered
all 40 items a second time in the suite's own order and returned the same
option 40 times out of 40. Running the permutation probe across seed blocks,
which is the obvious way to mirror the accuracy measurement in
[`2026-09-19-seed-variance.md`](2026-09-19-seed-variance.md), would have
reported a standard deviation of exactly zero and a floor of zero. That
number would have looked like rigor and been a property of the estimator.
The axis that carries the variance here is the one being perturbed.

**The published number is one draw from fifteen, and the spread is about as
large as the number.** On the base door the fifteen pairs run from 0.025 to
0.175 around a mean of 0.112, with a standard deviation of 0.0417. Reversal,
the pair every published flip rate used, returns 0.125, and six of the
fifteen pairs return less. It is an ordinary draw rather than an extreme
one, and it has no claim on being the answer: `021` against `102` is the
same kind of perturbation on the same door and the same items, and it
returns 0.025.

**One door's own flip rate carries about plus or minus 0.13.** Combining the
order axis and the item axis in quadrature, the base door's 0.125 at the
published pair is 0.125 plus or minus 0.134 at two sigma, and the
permutation adapter's 0.050 is 0.050 plus or minus 0.087. The base's
interval includes zero. A flip rate on 40 items is a count of five, and a
count of five does not measure a rate to three decimal places.

**0.120 against 0.040 does not survive.** That comparison is the band
adapter against the permutation-augmented one — 6 flips against 2 on 50
items — and it is the main published evidence that option-order augmentation
was worth doing. Measured here on the 40 of those items the three-way suite
leaves readable, the same pair of orders gives 5 flips against 2: a
difference of 0.075 against a two-sigma floor of 0.145. Over all fifteen
pairs the difference falls to 0.022 against a floor of 0.066. The counts say
it plainly. At the reversed pair the two doors disagree on seven items, five
where only the band adapter flips and two where only the permutation adapter
does, which an exact paired test puts at p = 0.45. That is a coin.
**The claim is withdrawn.**

**Nothing else in the table clears either.** All six two-door comparisons are
inside the noise on all three statistics: at the published pair, over all
fifteen pairs, and on the unstable share over six orders. The largest is the
base against the permutation adapter, +0.060 over all pairs against a floor
of 0.079 — 1.5 sigma, and the closest thing to a result on this page.

**The ordering is consistent, and consistency is not significance.** All
three statistics rank the doors the same way: the base is worst, the choice
and band adapters sit together, and the permutation adapter is lowest. That
is suggestive, and it is not more evidence, because the three statistics come
from the same 1,120 calls on the same 40 items. Three readings of one
measurement are one measurement.

**What it would cost to settle.** The item axis dominates, and it shrinks
with the square root of the item count. Resolving the base against the
permutation adapter at two sigma — a difference of 0.060 over all pairs
against a standard deviation of 0.0395 on 40 items — needs about **70 Choice
items**, which is 420 greedy calls a side at six orders each. Resolving the
band adapter against the permutation adapter, a difference of 0.022, needs
about **360**, nine times the Choice items this suite has. The first is an
afternoon of authoring. The second is the price of the sentence that was
published, and it is the reason the sentence should not have been.

**Measure over every order, not over one pair.** Six orders cost six calls an
item instead of two and remove the order axis entirely, because fifteen pairs
out of fifteen is the population rather than a sample of it. On the base
door that halves the two-sigma band on the rate itself, from 0.134 at one
pair to 0.069 over all fifteen; on the permutation adapter it goes from 0.087
to 0.050. It is the cheapest available improvement to this measurement, and
it should be the default the next time a flip rate is published.

**The cross-model comparisons are not like for like.** kev-0.5b's 7.4% is an
argmax flip rate over four orders on kev's own suite; Lev's 0.120 was over
two orders on this one. This run shows how much that matters: on the same
door and the same items, the share of items that move under *any* of six
orders is about twice the average two-order flip rate — 0.225 against 0.112
on the base, 0.100 against 0.052 on the permutation adapter. So "Lev flips
twice as often as kev" and "Lev's adapted flip rate is now below kev's" both
compare statistics that were never defined the same way.

**The Gym's probe now measures something else again.** `gym permute` reaches
a door over HTTP, and `lev-serve` serves the L2 estimator, so its flip rate
counts the argmax of an eight-sample ensemble changing rather than a greedy
answer changing. That statistic has two axes, the option order and the seed
block, and no number on this page or its siblings was produced that way. The
next run that uses it needs its own floor, and this one does not transfer.

**A sweep that drops calls silently is worse than no sweep.** The first
four-door run of this measurement shared one pool of helpers across every
door. A helper that serves one adapter and is then handed another keeps both
resident, and partway through the third door the runtime started refusing
calls. The tables came out clean: the band and permutation adapters each
reported a flip rate of 0.000 across all fifteen pairs, over zero answered
items. That is a false result that reads exactly like a strong one, and it
pointed the same way as the claim this run withdraws. The sweep now gives
each door its own pool and prints a refusal count per door; the run above
reports 0 of 280 for all four.

## What this does not say

One machine, one base model build, one authored suite, 40 items, one question
family, greedy decoding only. Ten of the 50 Choice items the published
numbers used are missing here: the three-way suite locked them, and a noise
measurement is not worth spending a locked partition on.

The floor belongs to this statistic on this item set. A suite with more
options per item has more orders and a different spread, and a door whose
flips concentrate on a few items has a different one again — they do
concentrate, and nine items carry every flip the base door makes.

Nothing here says the permutation augmentation failed to reduce order
sensitivity. It says this suite cannot tell, and it names the suite size that
could.
