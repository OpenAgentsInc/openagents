# Verse OS (spec, draft)

Written 2026-11-02. Status: proposal. Most of the parts exist and have run on
`coderos-4080`; the system that puts them together does not exist yet.

Verse OS is the operating system that CoderOS becomes. You turn the computer
on, and the first thing you see is the Verse, drawn by Aethyr on the GPU,
straight to the screen. There is no login screen, no desktop and no browser
in between. Your identity, your wallet, your agents and your programs are
inside the world. The whole machine belongs to the session: the display, the
input, the sound, the GPU, and the idle compute that earns you bitcoin.

It should feel like booting a starship, not a PC.

## What it is, in one picture

```
power on
  └─ firmware ─▶ systemd-boot (a Verse OS generation per entry)
       └─ Linux 6.18 + NixOS (nixpkgs, systemd, drivers)        ← kept, not rewritten
            └─ verse-session on tty1  (one Coax program, one process)
                 ├─ display:  Vulkan on the connector (VK_KHR_display), no compositor
                 ├─ input:    evdev keyboard, mouse, pads; camera hands; voice
                 ├─ world:    Aethyr + the realm (the Grid first), the HUD
                 ├─ agents:   Coder, Claude Code, Codex as presences in the world
                 ├─ inference: Psionic in the same process, same GPU
                 └─ windows:  Linux programs as panels in the world (a Wayland server inside)
```

Everything above the kernel and nixpkgs that we write is Coax, under the
rules in AGENTS.md.

### 3. Verse native on the GPU (`verse/gpu/`, `verse/native/`, `verse/kms/`)

[docs/verse/native-gpu.md](../verse/native-gpu.md) is the port that makes

The pieces Verse OS's session is made of:

  dumb buffers and a CPU rasterizer, 2026-10-30.
- `verse/gpu/vk-display.c`, `verse/gpu/display.sh`: **the boot session's
  core.** It switches to a free VT, takes DRM master, hands the connector to
  Vulkan (`VK_EXT_acquire_drm_display`), and presents with `VK_KHR_display` at
  the mode's rate. 1,196 frames, 0 dropped, validation 0/0. The status line
  ends: "Booting a machine straight into this (a session entry in the host's
  NixOS config) is not wired." Verse OS is that wiring, and everything after it.
- `verse/gpu/vk-window.c`: the same realm as a Wayland window, for running
  Verse inside a desktop.
- `verse/gpu/vkp.h`, `vkr.h`, `vk-realm.c`, `realm.sh`: the shared presenter,

- `verse/kms/evdev.h`, `keys.h`, `mice.h`: input with no compositor.
- `verse/native/realm-host.c`: what `host.js` does in the browser, natively.

And the world it opens on:

- `aethyr/` and its book: the engine core (loop, input, replay, entities,

- `aethyr/ui/hud.coax`, [the HUD library](../verse/2026-11-01-hud-library.md)

- [Aethyrforge](../verse/aethyrforge.md): making realms by voice, hand or agent.

## Principles

1. **The engine is the session.** One process owns the screen from the first
   frame after the kernel's. Not a game launched on a desktop; the desktop is
   a thing inside the game.
2. **Coax above nixpkgs.** NixOS stays as the base: kernel, drivers, systemd,
   packages, generations and rollback. Everything we write is Coax. Don't
   replace nixpkgs and don't write kernel modules (the audit's
   "What not to do" holds).
3. **There is always a way out.** A held key, a VT switch and the previous
   boot generation each get you to a working machine. Verse OS never traps
   its owner.
4. **Rules are checked code.** Device grants, admission of programs into the
   world, power limits, payment and update trials are `#authority` Coax with
   zero surviving mutants.
5. **Bitcoin only, keys local.** The identity and the wallet are made on the
   machine and never leave it unencrypted. No account is needed to boot.
6. **Same realm everywhere.** A realm that runs in Verse OS runs unchanged on
   openagents.com and in the iOS app. Verse OS adds a host, not a fork.

## The experience

### Boot

1. **Power on.** systemd-boot shows Verse OS generations, newest first,
   with a two-second timeout. No splash from anyone else.
2. **First light.** `verse-session` starts as soon as the GPU driver and
   `logind` are up, before the network. It takes tty1 through `logind`'s
   seat (the Coax compositor already takes the screen without root this
   way), and its first frame is the boot sequence: black, a grid line drawn
   across the screen, the HUD frame assembling around it (the ARWES-style
   frame and text animations), then system lines as they come true
   ("GPU RTX 4080 / 16 GB", "WALLET SYNCED", "PYLON ONLINE"). These are real
   events, not decoration; a line that fails stays red and says why.
3. **Drop in.** The camera falls from orbit to you on the Grid
   (`verse/home.coax`'s dolly), and you have control. Target: under 10
   seconds from the firmware's hand-off to control on `coderos-4080`.

### First boot

Same as above, then a short sequence in the world, by voice or keys:

- **Name yourself.** A Nostr identity is made (NIP-06). The seed words are
  shown once, behind a warning, and never logged.
- **Wallet.** A Spark wallet is made from it. Its balance sits in the HUD.
- **Your machine.** The session reads what the machine has (GPU, memory,
  disk) and says what it can do: which realms run at full rate, which models
  Psionic can serve, what the Pylon could earn.
- **Agents.** Sign in to the agents you use (Claude Code, Codex), or skip.

Nothing in the first boot needs the network except the wallet sync and the
agent sign-ins, and both can wait.

### Every boot after

You resume where you stood, in the realm you were in, with the agents that
were working still working (their state is in the session's record, as
`aethyr/replay.coax` records the player's).

### Living in it

- **The Grid is the home.** The Pylon terminal ahead of the spawn is the
  machine's console: a shell, the system's status, the logs.
- **Travel.** The galaxy (`verse`) opens every realm in `realms.json`;
  Aethyrforge builds new ones in place.
- **Agents are presences.** Each running agent (Coder, Claude Code, Codex,
  a child the main session spawns) is a figure in the world with a panel
  over it showing its transcript and its diff. CoderOS's agent panes
  (`agent-panes.nix`) and notices (`agent-notices.nix`) become this.
  Walk up to one and talk to it; voice goes through the voice engine.
- **Programs are panels.** Any Linux program (a browser, an editor, Zoom,
  Steam) runs as a Wayland client of the session, and its window is a panel
  in the world: pinned in space, held in your view, or full-screen. This is
  the audit's observation made literal: "a compositor is an engine that
  draws other programs' buffers as textures." `os/compositor.coax` already
  speaks the Wayland protocols and Xwayland; Verse OS moves its server into
  the session and replaces its 2D placement (`os/wm.coax`) with placement
  in the world.
- **Flat mode.** One key (`Super + Escape`) folds the world away and shows
  the panels tiled as the CoderOS desktop showed them, using `os/wm.coax`.
  For work that wants a plain screen. The same key returns.
- **Hands and voice.** The camera daemon's hand landmarks (`os/hands.coax`)
  and the voice engine are input like the keyboard: point, pinch, grab a
  panel, speak to an agent. Dictation (`os/dictate-toggle.coax`) types into
  the focused panel.
- **The machine works while you don't.** When you aren't playing, the idle
  GPU serves models through Psionic as a Pylon, within the power limits of
  `os/cpu-limits-policy.coax` and a matching GPU policy. Earnings arrive in
  the wallet, and the HUD shows them.
- **Record anything.** The screen recorder (`os/screen-record.coax`) and
  presentation mode are verbs in the world, not separate tools.

### Getting out

- `Ctrl + Alt + F2` … `F6`: text consoles, always (the session drops DRM
  master and the input grab on a VT switch, as `os/compositor.coax` does
  since `e3fe48cf88`).
- Holding `Escape` for three seconds ends the session to a console.
- A watchdog (as `verse/gpu/display.sh` has) ends a session that stops
  presenting frames, and the console takes the screen.
- The boot menu always has the previous generation, and one entry that
  boots to the CoderOS desktop instead of the session.

## The parts

### `verse-session`

One Coax program, one process. It is what `vk-display.c` is today, grown:

| Part | From | New work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display | `verse/gpu/vk-display.c`, `vkp.h` | Take the seat through `logind` (no root, no `display.sh` VT dance); more than one connector; hotplug |
| Input | `verse/kms/evdev.h`, `keys.h`, `mice.h` | libinput for touchpads and pads; hands and voice as `Intent`s (`aethyr/input.coax`) |
| World | `verse/gpu/vk-realm.c`, `vkr.h`, `verse/native/realm-host.c` | Persistent session state; the boot sequence as a realm |
| HUD | `aethyr/ui/hud.coax` | The system HUD: clock, wallet, Pylon, agents, notices |
| Wayland server | `os/compositor.coax`, `os/xwm.coax` | Client buffers imported as Vulkan textures (dmabuf); placement in the world; flat mode with `os/wm.coax`; the desk socket (`os/desk.coax`'s protocol) |
| Inference | `psionic/` | Same process, same GPU allocation as the renderer (the audit's "engine and inference in one process") |
| Audio | `verse/voice.coax`, `verse/platform/voice.c` | PipeWire output for realms and panels; ducking under voice |

The engine logic is Coax. The C host does the effects only: Vulkan, DRM,
evdev, PipeWire, sockets, dmabuf.

### The host description

As the audit's "The host description" proposes: which capabilities a
machine turns on, which devices go to which program, power limits and update
rules are a Coax file with `#test`, `#forall` and `#authority` checks.
`coax run` prints JSON, and one fixed NixOS module reads it with
`builtins.fromJSON`. The Nix is a stable adapter; the decisions are checked
code.

### Updates

`os/coax-update.coax` already builds, checks, tries, switches and rolls back
the machine's Coax programs under `os/coax-update-policy.coax`. In Verse OS
it refreshes the generated C from `main`, runs a NixOS build, compares the
closure, and switches. The session shows the update as a HUD line and never
restarts itself while you are in it; the new session starts at the next
boot or when you ask.

### Base system: NixOS builds the image, users never see Nix

Decided 2026-11-02. NixOS stays, as the tool that builds Verse OS, not as
the system a user configures.

**Why NixOS stays:**
- It already works: `coderos-4080` builds from it, the NVIDIA driver and
  CUDA 13.2 are packaged there, and every Coax program in `os/` installs
  through it.
- Every rebuild is a boot entry, which the "always a way out" principle
  depends on.
- Builds are reproducible, as Coax builds are (spec §16.1).
- The alternatives cost more: Arch or Debian with our packages on top lose
  rollback, and a distro of our own is years of packaging with no product
  behind it (audit, "What not to do").

**What NixOS is not, for Verse OS:**
- A user never sees a configuration file, `nixos-rebuild` or the Nix
  language.
- A user's machine never evaluates or builds Nix. Building on the machine
  is slow and can fail halfway; a machine people simply turn on gets a
  finished image swapped in whole.
- Nix stays the one layer we write that isn't Coax, and it doesn't grow.

**How it works:**

1. **Now (V0 to V6, `coderos-4080`):** plain NixOS, plus a module that boots
   straight into `verse-session`. The Verse OS modules live in this
   repository, in `os/nix/` (owner, 2026-11-02: "it must be in this
   openagents monorepo"). The edit loop: build, `nix store diff-closures`
   against `/run/current-system`, switch only with the owner's yes.
2. **Public (V7):** Nix builds a locked-down image on our own machines. It
   holds the kernel, the GPU drivers, systemd and `verse-session`, and
   leaves out the parts of NixOS a user would configure. The root
   filesystem is read-only. A machine keeps two copies of the system, A and
   B. An update downloads a finished image into the unused copy (through
   `systemd-sysupdate`, driven by `os/coax-update.coax` and its `#authority`
   policy) and boots it next time. If the new image fails to reach control
   in the Grid, the machine boots the old one again. SteamOS and ChromeOS
   work this way. User data (identity, wallet, realms, agent state) lives on
   a separate, encrypted partition the image never touches.
3. **The decisions are Coax.** The host description (devices to programs,
   power limits, update rules) is checked Coax that prints JSON; Nix only
   turns that JSON into the image.

**Where the Nix lives (owner, 2026-11-02):** in this monorepo, `os/nix/`.
It is the one place Nix comes back after the Nuke, and it stays small: the
session module, the host description adapter and the image. Personal values
(SSH keys, the hardware file, private modules such as Zoom or Battle.net)
stay out of it; a host imports the public flake and adds them. CoderOS's
`~/coder/os` keeps building `coderos-4080` until Verse OS's flake builds the
same machine.

### Hardware

- **First:** `coderos-4080` (RTX 4080, NVIDIA's open kernel module and
  closed userspace). Everything above has run there.
- **Next:** AMD and Intel GPUs through Mesa, the only fully open path. The
  session needs Vulkan 1.1 with `VK_KHR_display`; nothing NVIDIA-specific.
- **Apple:** not an OS target. The Mac and iPhone get the Verse as an app
  (`apps/openagents-ios`, Metal), the same realms, the same identity.

## Status

- **2026-11-02: the Verse in a window on the old CoderOS.** `coderos-4080`
  still runs a CoderOS that won't be updated, so the first step needs no
  system change. `verse/gpu/vkw.h` gives `vk-realm` a Wayland window
  (`--window`, `--fullscreen`): an xdg-shell toplevel with a Vulkan surface,
  the seat's keys shifted as evdev's are, and the pointer scaled to the
  realm's draw size. On the coder-compositor desktop the Grid ran at 59.86
  frames a second on a 59.951 Hz display, 0 dropped, 99th percentile 17.8 ms.
  `verse/gpu/realm.sh local window` builds and runs it with travel, on the
  machine itself (`local` skips ssh).
- **Online crash fixed.** With `--net`, every native realm that loads the
  homepage cache crashed on its first frame: `home_cache_seed` and the
  storage keys in `coax/web/project/landing-cache-face.c` passed stack
  buffers as 32-bit pointers, and natively the stack is above 4 GB. They
  are static now. The Grid then ran 1,200 frames online.
- **V0 pieces, built, not yet on a VT.** `vk-realm --leave-to N` holds its
  VT in graphics mode and shows VT N on leaving (or on a crash).
  `os/verse-session.coax` (13 checks) waits for its VT, runs the realm,
  follows travel, and resumes; `--window` runs the same loop on the desktop,
  which is how the Grid is open now. `realm.sh local install DIR` keeps the
  build, with GC roots for the libraries it links. `os/nix/` holds the
  `verseos.session` module and an example host that evaluates. Turning it on
  for `coderos-4080` is in NEEDS_OWNER.md.
- **Compiler on Linux.** A Linux release build of `coax` is 23 MB with
  symbols. `aethyr/render/build.coax` used to read the compiler whole into a
  4 MiB buffer to hash it, so it refused that build; it now hashes the file
  in 1 MiB chunks up to 256 MiB, and the hash matches `sha256sum`. The realms
  had only ever been generated on the Mac before.

- **V1: the boot sequence and resume (2026-11-02).** `os/verse-boot.coax`
  reads the machine when a session starts (the GPU from the NVIDIA driver or
  the card's PCI vendor, the first connected display and its mode, memory,
  the first physical link that is up, the CLI's Nostr key, a running Pylon)
  and writes them as `boot=LABEL|VALUE|STATE` facts. The Grid draws them
  (`apps/openagents-web/newland-boot.coax`): black, a line across the
  screen, the VERSE OS frame, each line typed in with READY, FAILED or
  WAITING, then the world and its glass panels fading in. With no boot facts
  (the website) nothing changes; the Grid's 118 checks pass. On
  `coderos-4080` it reads "NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 / DP-1 2560x1440 / 126 GB
  / link up on wlo1 / no identity yet / Pylon idle". `verse-session --state`
  keeps the realm you were in and starts there next time; the realm keeps
  your place in it (`vk-realm --net`). `coax/platform/store-file-native.c`
  now reads a file that reports size 0 to its end (up to 1 MiB), so
  `/proc` files read. Still open for V1: the firmware-to-control time,
  which needs a real boot into the session (NEEDS_OWNER.md).

- **V2: identity in the world (2026-11-02); the wallet waits.** With no
  key on the machine, the Grid says "NO IDENTITY ON THIS MACHINE · PRESS N
  TO MAKE ONE" after the boot sequence. N navigates to
  `/realms/verse-os-identity`; `verse-session` runs the Coax CLI's
  `openagents nostr new` (installed beside it by `realm.sh install`), keeps
  the report in memory, and writes the boot lines, `seed=` and `npub=` to
  `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/verse-identity.facts` (tmpfs, the user's alone). The
  Grid opens again with `vk-realm --facts-once`, which reads that file,
  removes it, and wipes its own copy and the realm's inbox. The words show
  once over a dimmed world behind a warning; Enter zeroes them and puts the
  npub on the IDENTITY line. Checked headless (N at frame 400 leaves with
  `verse-os-identity`), on the desktop with placeholder words (the file was
  gone within a second), and against a throwaway HOME for the CLI's report
  (12 words, `npub1`, a 0600 key file). The Spark wallet runs only inside
  the iOS app (`docs/lightning/`); on Linux it needs an SDK binding, which is
  the owner's decision (NEEDS_OWNER.md).

- **V4: your machine's agents in the Grid (2026-11-02).**
  `os/verse-agents.coax` looks every five seconds at where Claude Code,
  Codex and Coder write their transcripts and counts one written in the
  last two minutes as at work, in the last half hour as waiting (names and
  times only; nothing a transcript says is read). `vk-realm --agents FILE`
  sends the list as message 610 when it changes, and the Grid
  (`apps/openagents-web/newland-local.coax`) stands them in an arc seven
  metres ahead of the spawn, facing you, at work with a hand raised, named
  "CLAUDE CODE · openagents" and "AT WORK" or "WAITING 12 MIN". On
  `coderos-4080` it showed this session and the other one working in this
  repository. Still open: their transcripts and diffs on a panel, and
  talking to one, which wait on V3's panels and the voice engine.
- **V5: the Pylon while you are away (2026-11-02), off by default.**
  `os/verse-pylon-policy.coax` (`#authority`, 5 checks, 18 mutants, 0
  survivors): never unless `--pylon`; start after ten minutes with no key
  or mouse; stop the moment you touch one; five minutes' rest between a
  stop and the next start. `vk-realm --activity FILE` marks your input;
  `verse-session` ticks once a second and runs `openagents pylon start
  --model M` or `stop`. A live run against a stand-in CLI started at 601 s
  and stopped at 631 s, a second after the activity file was touched. Still
  open: Psionic in the session's own process (it runs as the CLI's join
  today), a power ceiling for the GPU like the CPU's, and earnings in the
  wallet (V2's wallet decision).
- **V7: the image, built by Nix (2026-11-02), not yet booted.**
  `os/nix/modules/verse-os-image.nix` is the appliance: systemd-boot and a
  UKI on the ESP, a read-only root in slot A, slot B and an encrypted
  `/home` made by `systemd-repart` on first boot, `systemd-sysupdate`
  filling the slot not in use from `verseos.image.updateUrl`, and the
  Verse on tty1 for `pilot`. `os/nix/pkgs/verse-realms.nix` builds
  `realm.sh local ship` output the way realm.sh does; built here from the
  Grid and the galaxy, the store's Grid ran 120 frames headless with every
  library found. `verse-session --run DIR` keeps what it writes out of a
  read-only store. The image evaluates (`nix flake check ./os/nix`); the
  disk was not built (several GB, on a machine that is doing other work),
  no version is published for sysupdate, and booting it on a second
  machine is the owner's (NEEDS_OWNER.md).

- **V3: your desk as a panel (2026-11-02).** Linux programs run on the
  Coax compositor (`os/compositor.coax`, Xwayland, `os/wm.coax` tiling),
  headless, on `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/wayland-verse`. `vk-realm --desk DIR`
  starts it, asks it for what changed (`frame PATH`, its test channel) up
  to thirty times a second while the panel shows, uploads the picture and
  blits it over the realm's frame (`verse/gpu/vkdesk.h`, a presenter overlay
  hook in `vkp`). F9 docks the desk at the right of the screen with the
  realm playing beside it; F10 is flat mode, the desk on the whole screen;
  while it shows the keyboard is the desk's (`keydown`) and the pointer over
  it is the desk's (`move`, `button`). `os/verse-panel.coax` (4 checks)
  decides all of it. On `coderos-4080` a `foot` terminal ran docked over
  the Grid at 59.6 fps. `realm.sh install` and the Nix package build the
  compositor and its launchers (`desk/launch/start`, `coder`, `pane`: a
  terminal; Super+Return opens another). Still open: windows placed in the
  world in 3D (the panel is held in your view), windows that outlive travel
  (the compositor is the realm's child and restarts with it), dmabuf
  instead of a PPM over tmpfs, the desk socket (`os/desk.coax`), and the
  wheel.

- **V6: your hand as the pointer (2026-11-02).** `vk-realm --hands
  SOCKET` reads the camera daemon's hands lines (`verse-session` passes
  `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/coderos-camera/hands.sock` when it exists) and gives
  the realm or the desk's panel the pointer events a mouse would
  (`verse/gpu/vkhands.h`). `os/verse-hands.coax` (4 checks) decides: the
  index fingertip, mirrored, the middle 70% of the camera's view spanning
  the screen, eased a fifth of the way each frame, and a pinch held two
  frames as the button, let go after three without one. Against a
  stand-in socket the pointer went from the middle to 14% across as
  computed, and the pinch pressed and released once. Not done: the daemon's
  hands were left off (turning them on uses your camera), so no real hand
  has driven it yet; and grabbing a panel to move it, which waits on panels
  placed in the world.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **The name.** Verse OS replaces the name CoderOS. (Where the Nix lives
   is settled: `os/nix/` in this repository, and Verse OS is published as
   an image. See "Base system".)
2. **V0 on `coderos-4080`.** Booting into the session changes the machine's
   default session. The CoderOS desktop stays in the boot menu.
3. **Who it is for first.** Your machine only, or anyone with a supported
   GPU from V7. That decides how much of first boot (identity, wallet,
   agent sign-in) must be polished before V7.
4. **The `sched_ext` scheduler** (audit, step 3): a Coax BPF scheduler for the
   frame-plus-inference mix. Worth a spike once V5 shows the contention.
