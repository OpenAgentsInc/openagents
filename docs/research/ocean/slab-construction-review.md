# SLAB construction film and frame review

The first two caissons tell the construction story best. At **00:30.167** a single concrete box is beside the work gantry for its first moorings. At **00:40.542** a second box meets it and the seam operation begins. The distinction matters. The first operation has to hold a free-floating body on station. The second also has to control relative motion between two bodies. The film shows their positions and sequence. Joint loads, line tension and a watertightness test result would require separate evidence.

[Watch the 92-second recording](slab-construction.mp4) · [Step through all 2,208 frames](slab-construction-frames.html). To use the reviewer offline, download the HTML and MP4 into the same folder. The review opens on frame zero. The arrow buttons move by one encoded picture, and the findings menu jumps to the corresponding frame.

## What can be checked from the pictures

The counter records the first and second installations separately and reaches all 120 hull units before outfitting begins. The first Brownsville trip also has a consistent order on screen. The lighter loads at the wharf, departs fully loaded, reaches the berth and then empties. Frame 1943 still shows 6,250 t aboard. Frame 1967 shows zero. These readings establish what the simulation did with the cargo. A camera on the open side of the berth would be needed to see how it got onto the platform.

The final geometry permits one arithmetic check. The simulation assumes a rectangular 1,000 m × 600 m waterplane and seawater density of 1.025 t/m³. At the displayed 25.0 m draft, that gives 15.375 million tonnes of displaced water, exactly the value on the panel. Subtracting that draft from the assumed 40 m berth depth gives the displayed 15 m static clearance. The film supplies no bathymetric survey, dynamic tow response, storm load case or joint capacity. Site and engineering conditions are discussed in the [feasibility analysis](slab-image-feasibility.md).

## How the frames were reviewed

The recording comes from the SLAB realm at commit `a5aa19101b`, running the construction view in the repository’s muted, fixed-step WebGPU recorder. FFprobe found one H.264 video stream at 1280 × 720 and 24 fps, with 2,208 consecutive presentation timestamps in 92.000 seconds. Frame 0 is the first rendered step. The final commissioned state first appears at frame 2159 and remains for the last 49 frames.

Every encoded frame was decoded in presentation order. The reviewer contains one entry per frame with the rendered day and hour, the matched stage label, the read joined count, the gold route marker position, and the share of the scene that changed. During outfitting it also includes the lighter number and tonnes aboard. The text was read using glyph and label templates from the recording, checked against the simulation schedule, and inspected at transitions and where the readings disagreed. Visual interpretation used a one-second contact sheet across the full film and adjacent frames around the joins, discharge and camera jumps.

The image-change measure uses all 1280 columns and rows 70 through 565, between the overlays. It counts pixels whose 8-bit luma changed by at least 12 from the preceding decoded frame. The largest change occurs at **01:22.125**, when 49.27% of that area changes. This measures an edit-like jump in the view. It cannot distinguish vessel motion, camera motion, water animation or compression at individual pixels. The route percentage measures the on-screen strip from wharf to berth. Its spatial scale differs from the world camera’s compressed route.
