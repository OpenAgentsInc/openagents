# Episode 290: Coder v1, the Metaharness

Christopher launches Coder 1.0, OpenAgents' coding metaharness. A cheap System 1 sits in front of the coding agents you already use: it picks the agent and model for each step and keeps small work away from the big model. Then the episode covers what that does to cost and speed, the product, and how plugin authors get paid in Bitcoin.

## Show notes

**Coder 1.0: the Metaharness**

Coder is OpenAgents' coding agent for the terminal, with the same conversation on your phone and the web. It sits in front of the coding agents you already use, Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Grok, Devin and Cursor, and a cheap, fast System 1 decides what each step needs. Small work goes to small models, or to our own Microcoder, and only the hard parts reach the big model.

On 21 real issues from our repository, three runs each, using the same big model, putting System 1 in front cut the cost per accepted fix from $4.00 to $1.84 and the median time from 284 to 121 seconds. It accepted the same number of fixes (19 of 63) both ways.

Install:

- macOS and Linux: `curl -fsSL https://openagents.com/cli/install.sh | bash`
- Windows (PowerShell): `irm https://openagents.com/cli/install.ps1 | iex`

## Editorial notes (for the owner)

What I chose and why:

- **The title is "Coder v1, the Metaharness".** "Metaharness" is the word that explains the product: it sits in front of the harnesses. Section 3 defines it in three sentences, so the title isn't jargon by the time it matters.
- **One number, said once, with its conditions.** $4.00 → $1.84 and 284 → 121 s, on the same model and the same 21 issues, with the same success. I left out "2–8×" and "up to 2.7×": the bigger multiples need a smaller model or a weaker comparison, and one honest number lands harder.
- **Whiteboard pieces used:** the hook, the routing, the network and the first close. They carry the energy, and the new voiceover only fills in what the whiteboard doesn't say: the definition, the numbers, the product and the payments.
- **Cut from the whiteboard:**
  - "Convince the Pope … good luck with that, Anthropic" (1:40–1:51). It's a cheap shot at a company whose agent we route to on day one, and it costs credibility with exactly the people we want installing.
  - "Bringing back determinism … cheaper, faster, better" (1:27–1:40). Section 5 says it with numbers.
  - The second close (2:16). It narrows "all platforms" to three.
- **Cut from the previous draft:**
  - "It ends with OpenAgents Coder: the final form of the coding agent." It's a claim nobody can check, and section 3 makes the same point concretely.
  - "Coder is that which evolves." It's lovely but vague. If you want it, it fits as the last line before the end card.
- **Kept from the series:** the DevDay "zero" callback, because it's the reason the payments section exists.
- **Not mentioned, because they aren't shipped to everyone yet:** Code Graph (being built), cloud computers (admin-only), Video Lab and Game Lab (local), Solver in the cloud, and shared projects on the phone. Second look is off by default; it could get a line in a later episode.
