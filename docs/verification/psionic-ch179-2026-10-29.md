# Chapter 179: how far a metric moves by itself, 2026-10-29

Verified on macOS 26.4 arm64 (Apple M5 Max) with Apple clang 21.0.0, Python
3.15.1 and coax0 0.24.0. No model weights, GPU, network or upstream code were
used. The Elephant numbers come from chapter 12's recorded run,
`psionic/fixtures/quality/heldout-v1-elephant-cpu.txt`; nothing was rerun.

`psionic/lib/noise.coax` implements profile `cpu-f64-noise-v1`:
- binary64, chapter 3's compensated accumulator for every sum;
- scores where higher is better, losses entered negated;
- up to 4,096 items in up to 1,024 tasks, with a partition per item;
- a noise floor of 1.96 × √2 standard deviations of 2 to 16 equal blocks of
  runs;
- a paired bootstrap over whole tasks, 2 to 10,000 replicates drawn by
  SplitMix64 at a counter, reporting the gain, its standard error and the
  replicates at or below a floor;
- a partition refused when its room below the ceiling is at most the floor,
  with room exactly 0 when every item is at the ceiling;
- per-item Brier score and first-maximum correctness;
- the synthetic world `noise-sim-v1`: 40 tasks of 10 items, tasks 32 to 39
  five logits easier, a per-run shift of up to 0.3 logits and a per-run task
  effect of up to 3.

## What mutation testing changed

The first run left five survivors, all in the checks:
- **A tolerance helper** whose `<` and `<=` were the same was removed; every
  check compares exactly, and the bootstrap check sums squares with the same
  accumulator as the library.
- **The world check** fills its output first, so skipping item 0 shows.
- **Item means** are checked for a suite of one item, and for a NaN in the
  first run of a later range.

## Limits

- No Elephant seed blocks: a repeated Elephant run would vary option order or
  wording, at over an hour per decision on the CPU reference.
- The floor assumes normally distributed block means; the self-comparisons
  suggest the 16-block estimate is about 12 percent low.
- No percentile intervals or verdicts (chapter 180), no suite digests
  (chapter 178).
