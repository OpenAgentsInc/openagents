**A protocol finding.** 25910, 26910 and 27010 are ephemeral (NIP-01's
20000–29999), and our relay stores none of them (`store_admit`:
`NS_ADMIT_EPHEMERAL`). A job sent while no Pylon is subscribed reaches
nobody, and an answer sent while the buyer is not subscribed is lost; the
relay's `--store` keeps the beacon and the relay list across the restart,
not jobs. So "a job sent during the outage is answered after it" holds when
the buyer resends the same signed job once the Pylon's beacon is back, which
any buyer without an answer must do anyway. The Pylon never decides a job
twice: a resent id is a duplicate in `relay.coax`'s and `pq_seen`'s windows,
and `pq_again` sends its kept answer again, the same event with the same id.

## What is missing (not claimed)

- One relay connection. Jobs are deduplicated by id in two 64-id windows
  and answers kept for the last 8; a relay replaying more than that could
  get a second answer, and nothing survives a restart of the Pylon itself.
- Ephemeral jobs: a job sent while the Pylon is away is answered only when
  the buyer resends it (see the finding above).
- At the original run, Apple TLS was a stub; this is superseded by the
  [Apple TLS follow-up](2026-10-30-apple-tls.md), whose macOS Pylon wss run
  passes 51 end-to-end checks under ASan/UBSan.
- One decision at a time over HTTP; a cancel for the running job is logged,
  not acted on. No `27010 processing` progress events.
- The execution receipt carries `attempt_id`, `schema` and `transport`, not
  the `request_digest` the deployed reader checks.
- The owner's NIP-OA `auth` tag on the beacon; idempotency storage across
  restarts; NIP-11 discovery of the relay's limits.
- `run.coax` has no `#test`s of its own: it is glue, and the end-to-end run
  is its check. Its decisions are in the mutation-gated units.
- No published event has left 127.0.0.1. Nothing was published to
  `relay.openagents.com` or any public relay.
