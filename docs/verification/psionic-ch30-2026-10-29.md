# Chapter 44: Elephant on CUDA, 2026-10-29

## What was run

The pinned original `baselabs/elephant-1` revision
`b3f8a93462fb1051a9de031e94a7ff380e254072`, authenticated by chapter 14's
session (SHA-256 of every artifact and the complete tensor manifest), on the
host of the [chapter 37 record](psionic-ch26-2026-10-29.md): an RTX 4080 with
driver 595.71.05, NVRTC 13.2.51 and coax0 0.23.0. The request is chapter 14's
sample: `Water is wet.`, `Is the water wet?`, options `yes` and `no`, 86
tokens with a 49-token prefix, temperature one.

- Kernels: `psionic/lib/device/project.coax` and `layer.coax` (17 kernels),
  compiled with `coax cuda --release` and NVRTC at launch.
- Plan: `psionic/lib/device/plan.coax` decides the workspace layout, the
  resident and streamed weights and every copy, wait, release and launch.
- Host: `psionic/tests/elephant_device.c` with `psionic/platform/cuda-host.c`,
  run by `psionic/tests/run_elephant_device.sh`.

All three Coax units are `#authority` with zero-survivor mutation runs
(project 231 mutants, layer 364, plan 1,678) and debug and release checks;
`psionic/tests/check_device.sh` is the focused gate.

## Acceptance test, frozen before the first device run

The choice must be `yes`, and each of the four decision statistics
(probabilities for `yes` and `no`, slot mass and confidence) must be within
0.0005 absolute of chapter 14's independent PyTorch reference and of chapter
14's recorded CPU result (`psionic/fixtures/models/elephant/psionic-reference.txt`).
The CPU decision was not rerun.

| Statistic | Device | Recorded CPU | Independent reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| choice | yes | yes | yes |
| p(yes) | 0.757196636 | 0.757196285 | 0.757196248 |
| p(no) | 0.242803364 | 0.242803715 | 0.242803693 |
| slot mass | 0.228232783 | 0.228232722 | 0.228232533 |
| confidence | 0.514393272 | 0.514392571 | 2 p(yes) - 1 = 0.514392495 |

The largest difference from the CPU result is 7.0e-7 (confidence). The same
answer came back from every budget below.

## Per-kernel qualification on the original weights

With `PSIONIC_DEVICE_QUALIFY=1` the harness repeats each launch of layer 0
(the first linear-attention layer), layer 3 (the first full-attention layer)
and the final norm and readout on the CPU. The CPU runs the same Coax kernels
compiled to C, starting from a copy of the device workspace taken just before
the launch, and the two workspaces are compared word for word afterwards.

55 launches were compared. 54 left the whole 87 MB workspace identical bit for
bit, including every projection, norm, the convolution, the DeltaNet gates,
recurrence and head norm, attention preparation, scores and the attention sum.
The exception is `dl_softmax` in layer 3: 7,399 of its f64 output words differ
in low-order bits, because its `exp` is the CUDA math library's and not the C
library's. Its consumer, `dl_attend`, was identical given the device's own
probabilities.

## Time to response

Wall time from the host's embedding of the request to the option
probabilities, after the session is open, five repetitions in one process:
236.6, 236.6, 236.8, 237.4 and 238.5 ms (mean 237.2 ms). Of that, the device
time from the input upload to the logits download was 232.8-234.7 ms; the
input upload (86 embedding rows and rotary coefficients) took 0.14-0.23 ms
and the host's embedding, rotary table and readout the remaining 3-4 ms. In
other processes the first decision after opening took 237-258 ms.

The CPU reference path took 2,092 s for the same decision (chapter 14's
measurement), so the device answers about 8,800 times faster.

Every budget returned the same probabilities to all nine printed digits.
Streamed matrices are copied from pinned host memory through two 96 MiB slots
while earlier layers compute. Down to 12 GiB the copies are hidden; below
that PCIe becomes the limit: at 6.25 GiB the decision moves 9.5 GB in about
0.41 s, 23 GB/s. The plan refuses a budget below
about 6.1 GiB.

| Copy of 0.94 GiB of streamed weights into pinned memory | included in the upload line's process time |

Device memory in use by the process was 14.12 GiB at the default budget.

## Against cuBLAS (opt-in comparator)

`psionic/tests/bench_cublas.sh` runs the same shapes through cuBLAS 13.3.0.5,
loaded from `~/cublas-13.2` by the test harness only. Psionic's time as a
fraction of cuBLAS's (above 100% means Psionic is faster), and the
difference between the two results relative to `sum |w x|`:

| Shape (rows x cols x tokens) | Psionic | SGEMM, f32 weights | GemmEx, BF16 weights and activations | vs SGEMM | vs GemmEx | Difference from SGEMM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 248,320 x 4,096 x 1 | 3.46 ms | 6.19 ms | 3.12 ms | 179% | 90% | 4.3e-8 |
| 12,288 x 4,096 x 1 | 0.174 ms | 0.314 ms | 0.152 ms | 181% | 87% | 4.0e-8 |
| 4,096 x 12,288 x 1 | 0.178 ms | 0.314 ms | 0.151 ms | 177% | 85% | 3.4e-8 |
| 8,192 x 4,096 x 86 | 0.905 ms | 0.340 ms | 0.104 ms | 38% | 12% | 5.9e-8 |
| 12,288 x 4,096 x 86 | 1.31 ms | 0.473 ms | 0.204 ms | 36% | 16% | 4.5e-8 |
| 4,096 x 12,288 x 86 | 1.28 ms | 0.523 ms | 0.187 ms | 41% | 15% | 4.8e-8 |
| 4,096 x 4,096 x 86 | 0.486 ms | 0.163 ms | 0.052 ms | 34% | 11% | 6.6e-8 |
| 1,024 x 4,096 x 86 | 0.178 ms | 0.044 ms | 0.017 ms | 25% | 10% | 5.9e-8 |

For one token Psionic reads BF16 weights at 85-90% of cuBLAS's BF16 rate
(and beats f32 SGEMM, which reads twice the bytes). For the 86-token
products it reaches 34-41% of SGEMM on CUDA cores and 10-16% of the BF16
tensor-core GEMM. SGEMM agrees with Psionic within 6.6e-8 of `sum |w x|`, an
independent check of the same product; the BF16 GEMM differs by up to 1.7e-4
because it rounds the activations to BF16.
