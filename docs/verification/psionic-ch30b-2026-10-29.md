Against the floor: the driver-API test (54.7 GB of shard bytes through two
96 MiB slots, 2.31 s, 23.7 GB/s) predicts 1.60 s for 37.8 GB and 2.16 s for
51.2 GB. With most of the card the decision runs at the link's rate, 1.60 s,
30% under the 2.3 s all-bytes floor because 12.5 GiB stay resident. Fully
streamed it takes 2.63-2.84 s, 80% of the link's rate and 15-25% over the
floor; the gap was not profiled (candidates: 508 separate pinned
allocations, the 170 MiB slot copies, and host contention on a shared
machine).

The Lab request is five typed questions about `Water is wet.` sharing its
49-token prefix (86-87 tokens each). Answers, identical whole and forked:
`Is the water wet?` yes 0.944615702; `Is the message about water?` yes
0.97859031; `Is the message a question?` no (p_yes 0.00483335113); `Is the
message about fire?` no (0.0120048166); `Is the message true?` yes
0.909868535. Forking does not help a streamed Mammoth: each pass (prefix or
suffix) streams every streamed matrix again, so five forked questions are six
passes. With most of the card it is 19% slower; fully streamed, the same
within noise.

## Limits

One request shape, one machine, card free during each decision, clocks not
pinned. Per-kernel qualification against the CPU (`PSIONIC_DEVICE_QUALIFY` in
chapter 30) was not run for Mammoth; the kernels and their arguments are
chapter 30's, qualified on Elephant's weights, and the plan checks show the
Mammoth launches differ only in counts. The Elephant engine was not rerun on
the device after the change; its plans are identical digest for digest and the
host change is the kind lookup. A batched suffix pass, which would make a
streamed Lab request about two passes, and quantized weights are not built.
