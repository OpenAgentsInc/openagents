# Splat Lab: Gaussian splats in the Verse renderer

Splat Lab (`/worlds/splat-lab`, unlisted) tests 3D Gaussian splats in the
Verse's own WebGPU renderer. This note covers what works, what it costs, and
how splats could come to Everglade for the raid.

## What the lab is

A splat is a 3D Gaussian: a centre, a 3x3 covariance (its size, stretch and
turn), a colour and an opacity. To draw one, you project its covariance to a
2D ellipse on the screen, draw a quad over that ellipse, and fade the quad
with the Gaussian's falloff. Splats only look right when they're blended from
back to front, so every frame sorts them by depth.

The lab does all of this in Coax and WGSL, with no third-party JavaScript:

- `apps/openagents-web/splatlab.coax` holds the scenes, the camera, the HUD,
  the authored splats, and the `.splat` and `.ply` loaders.
- `apps/openagents-web/gpu/splatlab-fx.coax` makes each animated splat from
  its index and the time, sorts all of them, and projects each one to a 2D
  ellipse (EWA projection with the pinhole's Jacobian, a 3-sigma quad).
- `apps/openagents-web/gpu/splatlab.coax` draws the night sky, the meshes,
  the bloom and the final picture.
- `coax/web/host.js` runs the sort's passes before the frame ("Sorting" in its
  header), and reads GPU timestamps for the HUD when the page asks
  (`"timing": true` in `realms.json`).

| 2 | SPELLS | 32,768 | Fireball (flight, burst, smoke) and Eldritch Blast (a wound beam) |
| 3 | TOWN | 40,704 | Mesh cottages burning; splats share the meshes' depth buffer |
| 4 | SHRINE | about 110,000, or 262,000 with the skull | Darian's ritual site, authored in splats, plus a captured skull |
| 5 | BENCH | 16,384 to 1,048,576 | A static meadow, for splat count against frame rate |

The shrine's skull is the CC BY 4.0 Babylon.js capture `gs_Skull.splat`
(162,598 splats, 151,950 kept after dropping faint ones). It lives in a cache
outside the repository (`~/.openagents/verse/private-build/web/splats/`), and
only a local address loads it (the new `local:` asset kind). On
openagents.com the shrine stands without it.

## What works

- **A GPU sort without compute shaders.** The Coax WGSL backend writes vertex
  and fragment entries only, so the sort is a bitonic sort run as fragment
  passes over an `rg32float` texture: one pass writes each splat's key (minus
  its depth) and index, and each pass after it does one bitonic step, ping-
  ponging between two textures. N splats take 1 + log2(N)(log2(N) + 1)/2
  passes: 121 for 32,768 and 211 for 1,048,576. No CPU or worker sort is used.
- **Splats and meshes in one picture.** Splats test against the meshes' depth
  buffer and don't write it. A cottage wall hides the flames behind it, and
  smoke drifts in front of the roofs. This is the case the raid needs.
- **Animated splats cost no upload.** Fire, smoke, embers and spells are made
  on the GPU from each splat's index, its emitter (16 emitters in the uniform
  block) and the time. Only static splats (the shrine, a capture) live in a
  storage buffer, uploaded once when the scene changes.
- **Captured assets load.** The `.splat` loader (32 bytes a splat) and the
  3DGS `.ply` loader (exp scales, sigmoid opacity, SH degree 0 colour) both
  work, and both have tests.
- **Fire reads well as splats.** Stretched Gaussians along each tongue's
  flight, blended additively, look like flame without a texture.

## What it costs

Measured on this Mac (Apple M5 Max), headless Chrome, WebGPU on Metal. The
frame rate is capped at 60 by the display's refresh.

On a quiet GPU, desktop (1280 x 800):

| Scene | Splats | FPS | Sort | Draw |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FIRE (before the tongues) | 30,720 | 60 | 1.5 ms | 7.6 ms |
| SPELLS | 32,768 | 60 | 1.3 ms | 7.8 ms |
| TOWN | 40,704 | 60 | 1.6 ms | 8.4 ms |
| SHRINE (with skull) | 258,454 | 60 | 3.5 ms | 3.0 ms |
| BENCH | 262,144 | 60 | 2.3 ms | 1.3 ms |

On a busy GPU (other agents' headless pages and a visible tab of the lab
open), desktop and a 390 x 844 phone viewport at 3x:

| Splats | Desktop FPS, sort, draw | Phone FPS, sort, draw |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16,384 | 60, 15.6 ms, 0.4 ms | 60, 15.3 ms, 1.0 ms |
| 65,536 | - | 35, 21.9 ms, 1.0 ms |
| 262,144 | - | 31, 33.0 ms, 3.2 ms |
| 1,048,576 | 21, 44.3 ms, 9.0 ms | 18, 42.2 ms, 9.6 ms |

What the numbers say:

- **The sort, not the drawing, sets the price.** A fragment-pass sort costs
  about 200 render passes a frame, and each pass waits its turn on a shared
  GPU. On a quiet GPU it's cheap (2.3 ms for 262,144 splats). On a busy one
  the same sort takes 15 to 44 ms. A real phone's GPU is slower than this
  Mac's, so plan for the busy numbers.
- **Overdraw sets the drawing cost.** The fire's 30,720 splats cost more to
  draw (7.6 ms) than the bench's 262,144 (1.3 ms), because flame and smoke
  splats stack dozens deep over the same pixels while the meadow's splats
  each cover a few.
- **Memory:** 48 bytes a static splat on the GPU and in Wasm memory, plus an
  8-byte sort texel. The shrine with the skull uses about 13 MB of splats.

## What doesn't work yet

- **Authored solid objects look soft.** The standing stones and altar are
  close, but read as fuzzy compared with the mesh cottages. Splats suit
  captures, fire and smoke more than hard edges a designer makes by hand.
- **Lighting.** Splats carry their own colour (SH degree 0). They don't take
  the moon or firelight, so a capture looks lit the way it was when it was
  captured. Meshes take firelight from the splat fires; splats don't light
  each other.
- **No compute.** A compute radix sort would need tens of dispatches, not
  about 200 passes. That needs a compute and `read_write` storage path in the
  Coax WGSL backend.

## Recommendation for Everglade and the raid

1. **Bring splat fire and smoke to the raid first.** It is the strongest
   result here. A burning street of about 40,000 splats holds 60 fps on a
   quiet GPU and shares the depth buffer with Everglade's meshes, so the
   buildings can stay meshes. Port the tongue fire (`sl_flame`), smoke
   (`sl_smoke`) and embers (`sl_ember`) as an emitter kind beside
   `aethyr/fx/`, owned by whoever owns the fire effects.
2. **Cap and sort less often.** Keep a raid's live splats under about 64,000
   on phones, and sort every second frame when the camera is still
   (`coaxial_sort`'s fourth float already allows it). Smoke and fire tolerate
   a frame-old order.
3. **Use captures only for set pieces.** A captured relic, like the skull on
   Darian's altar, is worth its 150,000 splats in one scene, licensed CC0 or
   CC BY and kept out of the repository. Don't capture whole areas yet:
   without relighting, captured ground won't match Everglade's day and night.
4. **Add compute to the WGSL backend before going past about 250,000
   splats.** A compute radix sort is the step that makes a million splats
   practical on phones.

## Try it

- Local: `coax/web/serve.sh apps/openagents-web --listen 127.0.0.1:PORT`, then
  open `/worlds/splat-lab`. Add `#fire`, `#spells`, `#town`, `#shrine` or
  `#bench-0` to `#bench-3` to open a scene.
- Tests: `coax/bin/coax test apps/openagents-web/splatlab.coax` and
  `coax/bin/coax test apps/openagents-web/gpu/splatlab-fx.coax --library`.
