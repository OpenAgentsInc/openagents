# Belt 2200

Pitched by Ted on the October 30, 2026 recording: a real-time strategy game
in the style of hard sci-fi about the solar system, set in the year 2200, on
our engine.

Status: design only. Nothing is built.

## The game in one line

Run a faction in a settled solar system where ships obey real orbits, fuel
is everything, and every order you send arrives late, at the speed of light.

## Style and originality

Hard sci-fi in general: real orbital mechanics, people who live and work in
low gravity, water and air as the economy, slow ships and long silences. The
factions, ships, places, words and story are ours. None of the names,
factions, ships, slang or plot of any existing book or show.

## The setting

The year 2200. Three powers share the system:

- **Terran Compact:** Earth and the Moon. The biggest population and fleet,
  and dependent on imports of water ice and metals.
- **Mars Union:** a hard-working, terraforming republic with the best
  ships and the fewest people.
- **The Free Belt:** the asteroid miners and the outer moons' stations. Few
  warships, but they control the ice, the metal and the routes.

A fourth side is optional: **the Unknown**, something found in the outer
system that every faction wants to reach first. A match can be played
without it.

## How it plays

- **The map is the real solar system**, scaled for play: the Sun, the inner
  planets, the Belt as a field of named rocks, Jupiter's and Saturn's moons.
  Everything moves on its orbit as the match runs, so routes open and close.
- **Ships fly real orbits.** You don't steer; you plan a burn. Pick a ship,
  pick a destination, and the game shows the transfer: the burn, the coast
  and the arrival, with its fuel and its time. A fast route costs more
  reaction mass.
- **Time runs faster than real life and you can pause.** A turn is a day of
  game time a few seconds; a transfer to the Belt takes minutes. Single
  player can pause and plan; multiplayer runs at a fixed rate.
- **Light lag is the fog of war.** You see each place as it was when its
  light left. Orders to a far fleet take the light time to arrive. A
  commander's ship (a flagship with authority) acts on its own standing
  orders.
- **The economy:** water ice (becomes air, water and reaction mass), metals
  (ships and stations) and people (crews, colonists, workers). Mine at rocks
  and moons, refine at stations, ship it on freighters along routes that
  pirates and enemies can intercept.
- **Combat:** fleets meet when their paths cross. Point defense, missiles
  (that also burn and coast) and rail guns. Combat is a short tactical
  view: ships keep their momentum, and turning to fire costs time. Then
  the strategic map again.
- **Winning:** control the ice, starve a capital, or (with the Unknown) get
  there first and hold it.

## What the engine needs

1. **Orbital mechanics** (`verse/orbit.coax`, new): Kepler orbits for the
   bodies; patched conics for ships (sphere of influence handoff); the
   transfer planner (Lambert's problem) that turns "go there" into a burn,
   a coast and its fuel. All in f64, which `verse/rigid.coax` already uses.
2. **Huge scales.** The scene spans billions of kilometres and also shows a
   ship a few hundred metres long. A floating origin and camera-relative
   drawing, with logarithmic depth, so nothing jitters.
3. **Selection and orders UI** (`verse/hud.coax` and a new
   `verse/command.coax`): box select, groups, a command queue, the planned
   burn drawn on the map, and a timeline scrubber. On phones: tap select,
   long-press for orders (the hotbar's tap and long-press rules).
4. **Light-lag visibility**: each faction sees each object as it was at
   distance divided by c ago. Keep a short position history per object and
   answer "where did they see it" queries. This doubles as replay.
5. **Deterministic lockstep multiplayer**: the same orders give the same
   results on every client, so only orders cross the network. Fixed-point
   or carefully ordered f64, and a desync checker that compares state
   hashes every few seconds.
6. **Save and resume**: long campaigns need saves, as Fairy Forest does
   (`verse/fairy-forest/`, its save sections), only much bigger.
7. **AI opponents in two layers**: plain code for tactics (intercepts, point
   defense, routes) and the Decisions API for strategy (which route to
   open, when to press an attack), with every choice logged.
8. **Thousands of objects**: Belt rocks, freighters, missiles. Instanced
   drawing and simulation off the main thread.

## Milestones

1. **Orrery.** The real solar system moving, a camera that zooms from the
   Sun to a station, and floating origin. No play yet.
2. **One ship.** Plan a transfer, burn, coast, arrive. Fuel and time shown.
3. **Economy.** Mine ice, refine, ship it, build ships.
4. **War.** Fleets, intercepts, the tactical view.
5. **Light lag.** Delayed sight and orders; flagships with standing orders.
6. **Rivals.** AI factions, then lockstep multiplayer.
