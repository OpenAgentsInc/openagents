## Answer

Adoption means making an externally validated claim's subject part of the agent everyone starts with, on the strength of its marginal effect against the current default set, and keeping it there only while the claims it rests on still hold. It is a policy decision, not evidence. Adoption evaluates marginal capability, not historical capability: the current defaults plus the candidate against the current defaults alone.

## Details

- Standalone and marginal deltas come apart through redundancy, synergy, interference, routing competition, and context cost.
- Adoption evaluates marginal utility and marginal authority, with safety kept a constraint. Reliability is adoption evidence: consistency across repeats, robustness, calibration, tail failures, and reliability when composed. Stakes set the bar.
- A decision right 99 % of the time gives about a 90 % chance that ten independent decisions all come out right, and about 37 % for a hundred.
- Adoption is not terminal: when A, B, E, G, or M changes, the key changed and the claim is revalidated; the weaker the subject's identity, the faster the evidence expires.
